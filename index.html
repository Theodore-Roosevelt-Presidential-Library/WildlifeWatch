        Watch safely

        Watch Safely

        Every animal here is wild — even the ones that look calm on the side of the road. Give them room and they will give you a better show.

        How far is 25 yards?

        The National Park Service asks everyone to stay at least 25 yards from all wildlife. For bison, more is better.

        thumb_up

        The thumb test

        Stretch out your arm, give a thumbs-up and close one eye. If your thumb hides the whole animal, you're far enough back. If you can still see it around your thumb, you're too close — back away.

        undo

        If it moves, you moved too close

        An animal that stops eating, stares or walks away is telling you to back off.

        directions_car

        Your car is a blind

        Bison and horses often walk right past cars. Stay inside, pull fully off the road and let them pass.

        volume_off

        No calls, no spotlights

        Wildlife calls and spotlighting are not allowed in the park. They stress animals and change their behavior.

        pets

        Pets stay in the car

        Dogs can frighten wildlife and be hurt by it. Pets aren't allowed on park trails.

        visibility

        See one, look for more

        Drive slowly. Where there's one deer, elk, pronghorn or horse, there are usually others close by.

        grass

        Watch your step

        Prairie rattlesnakes rest in tall grass and near prairie dog towns. Stay on trails and look before you sit.

        block

        Never feed wildlife

        Feeding, chasing or harassing animals is illegal and dangerous — for you and for them.

        For web editors and partners

        Embed on Your Site

        Every section of Wildlife Watch can drop into any web page — trlibrary.com, a partner's site, a blog. Paste two lines and the widget sizes itself, stays up to date, and keeps its own styling.

        How it works

        1. Add the script once per page. It can go anywhere on the page, before or after the widgets.
        2. Add a placeholder wherever a widget should appear. Change the word in quotes to pick the widget. Use as many as you like on one page.
          <div data-wildlife-watch="horses"></div>
        3. Publish. The widget loads, matches its height to its content, and refreshes its data daily on its own.

        On trlibrary.com (Drupal)

        1. In Layout, add a Code (embed) block where the widget should go.
        2. Paste the full snippet for the widget (placeholder plus script) into the block's code field.
        3. Turn Display title off — each widget carries its own heading — and check Fit so it spans the column.
        4. Save the layout. If you add several widgets to one page, the script line can repeat safely.

        Live example: trlibrary.com/visit/wildlife

        Linking instead of embedding

        Every section has its own address, so you can link straight to it from a newsletter, social post or QR code:

          Add ?species=bison (or any animal name above) to open that animal's card directly, for example wildlife.labs.trlibrary.com/?species=horse.
